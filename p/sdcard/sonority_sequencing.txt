The Sonority Sequencing Principle (SSP) is a language agnostic algorithm proposed by Otto
Jesperson in 1904.
The sonorous quality of a phoneme is judged by the openness of the lips.
Syllable breaks occur before troughs in sonority.
For more on the SSP see Selkirk (1984).
The default implementation uses the English alphabet,
but the `sonority_hiearchy` can be modified to IPA or any other alphabet for the use-case.
The SSP is a universal syllabification algorithm,
but that does not mean it performs equally across languages.
Bartlett et al. (2009) is a good benchmark for English accuracy if utilizing IPA (pg.
311).
Importantly, if a custom hierarchy is supplied and vowels span across more than one level,
they should be given separately to the `vowels` class attribute.
